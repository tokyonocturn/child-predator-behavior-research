# Contributing

This repository is a public research resource. Contributions are welcome from researchers, clinicians, educators, safeguarding professionals, survivors, and informed readers. The standard is not "sounds right": it is "traceable to good evidence."

---

## 1. What we want

- **Corrections** to statistics, citations, dates, or factual claims.
- **Additional peer-reviewed sources**, especially systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and nationally representative studies.
- **Replications or contradictions** of claims already in the repository.
- **Prevention evaluations**, including null and negative results.
- **Regional data** from countries outside the U.S., U.K., Canada, and Australia.
- **Accessibility and clarity improvements** that do not change meaning.
- **Translations**, with a note on who reviewed the translation.

## 2. What we do not accept

- Material that identifies a victim, or that could reasonably be used to identify one.
- Accusations against named individuals who have not been convicted in a court of law.
- Operational guidance for offending, evading detection, or concealing abuse.
- Links to, or reproductions of, child sexual abuse material.
- Content that blames victims, families, or demographic groups for an offender's behavior.
- Unsupported claims presented as established fact, including the "father under 200 pounds" claim.
- Advocacy for violence, vigilantism, or "paedophile hunting."
- AI-generated citations that cannot be verified against a primary source.

If a contribution includes a concern about a specific child, **do not post it here**. Contact the appropriate authority. In the United States: Childhelp **1-800-422-4453**, NCMEC **1-800-THE-LOST**, or **911**.

---

## 3. Source standards

Every substantive claim should be supported by a source that a reader can check. Acceptable sources, in descending order of preference:

1. Peer-reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
2. Peer-reviewed primary studies in reputable journals.
3. Official statistical reports from government or intergovernmental bodies (CDC, FBI, DOJ/BJS, NCMEC, WHO, UNICEF, national statistical offices).
4. University or institutional research reports.
5. Practitioner monographs from recognized bodies (e.g., NCMEC behavioral analysis).
6. High-quality journalism **only** for tracing the origin of a claim, never as evidence that the claim is true.

For each source, provide:

- authors (or issuing organization),
- year,
- title,
- journal or publisher,
- DOI or stable URL,
- a one- or two-sentence annotation saying what it supports and what its limitations are.

If you cannot verify a source, say so. An unverified citation is worse than no citation.

---

## 4. Evidence grading

New claims should be labeled using the repository's grades: **Established**, **Emerging**, **Disputed**, or **Unsupported**. See [`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md) for the rubric.

If you are adding a claim that contradicts an existing grade, explain why. "This study found X" is not enough if the existing grade rests on a meta-analysis; explain the study's design, sample, and limitations, and whether it should change the weight of evidence.

---

## 5. Style and language

- Use person-first, non-stigmatizing language. "Person who sexually abused a child," not "monster" or "predator" as a default noun.
- Reserve "pedophilia" for the clinical construct. Do not use it as a synonym for child sexual abuse.
- Do not describe victim behavior as provocation, consent, or "asking for it."
- Distinguish correlation from causation explicitly.
- Avoid demographic stereotypes. If you report a group difference, explain the structural and measurement factors that may produce it.
- Use headings, short paragraphs, and tables where they aid comprehension.
- Use sentence case for headings (except proper nouns).
- Define acronyms on first use.

---

## 6. How to contribute

1. **Open an issue** describing the change and the evidence. For small typo fixes, a pull request without an issue is fine.
2. **Fork the repository** and create a branch with a descriptive name (e.g., `fix/recidivism-base-rate`).
3. **Make the change**, including the source and annotation in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` if you add a new source.
4. **Check your claims** against the primary source. Quote the relevant sentence if the claim is contested.
5. **Open a pull request** explaining what changed, why, and what evidence supports it.
6. **Respond to review.** A reviewer may ask for the primary source, a limitation statement, or a softer wording.

### Commit messages

Use descriptive, present-tense messages:

- `Add Australian disclosure data to statistics paper`
- `Correct recidivism follow-up period in offender psychology`
- `Clarify that household composition is correlational`

Avoid `update`, `fix`, or `wip`.

---

## 7. Review process

- At least one maintainer reviews each pull request.
- Claims that would change a headline finding require two reviews.
- Reviews check: source quality, accurate quotation, appropriate grade, non-stigmatizing language, and absence of operational detail.
- Disagreements about interpretation are resolved by presenting both positions, not by deleting the weaker one.
- A contributor may be asked to add a "Limitations" note rather than remove a claim.

---

## 8. Corrections and retractions

If a cited study is retracted, corrected, or superseded:

- open an issue with the retraction notice,
- the repository will remove or flag the claim,
- the commit message will state the correction.

We do not quietly delete inconvenient findings. We label them.

---

## 9. Licensing of contributions

By contributing, you agree that your contribution is licensed under **CC BY 4.0**, the same license as the repository. Do not contribute text or images you do not have the right to license.

---

## 10. Code of conduct

- Be precise, not combative.
- Critique claims, not people.
- Assume good faith, but require evidence.
- Do not use this repository to harass, dox, or accuse.
- Survivors and practitioners may have direct experience that informs their perspective; treat it with respect while still requiring empirical support for factual claims.

Maintainers may remove contributions that violate these standards.

---

## 11. A note on AI assistance

AI tools may be used to draft or organize contributions, but the contributor is responsible for verifying every citation and statistic against the primary source. Do not submit AI-generated references without checking that they exist, that the authors are correct, and that the quoted finding appears in the source. Fabricated citations are the fastest way to destroy a research repository's credibility.
